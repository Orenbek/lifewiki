# Composition and readability

## Establish the reading order

During editing, explain what obstacle to understanding each section resolves and why the next section follows. These notes need not appear in the article. Remove sections without a purpose, combine repeated explanations, and move material whose prerequisites are missing or supply the minimum necessary premise in place.

Reading only the headings and each section's opening and closing should reveal an explanatory sequence, not just a list of related topics. Start with an experience, question, concept, or conclusion according to the material and reader.

For example, an article containing contrasting experiences of persistence could change from definitions, experiences, benefits, and advice on persisting to why the author began questioning persistence, how two experiences differed, how to distinguish accumulation from repeated expenditure, and when to reconsider. This order is specific to the material. It must not become a universal template or presume persistence is good or bad.

## Edit paragraphs and transitions

- Give each paragraph a recognizable purpose while preserving natural variation in length and the author's rhythm. Do not require every paragraph to start with a topic sentence.
- Explain the reasoning behind consequential judgments and the relevance of experiences, quotations, and examples. Do not mechanically append a statement of what every paragraph demonstrates.
- Check for unsupported movement from observation to explanation to general judgment. A citation does not by itself justify a conclusion.
- Supply the minimum premises needed to understand the page. Link to deeper background with an explanation of its relevance instead of repeating a prerequisite article in full.
- Connect paragraphs through concepts, causes, comparisons, or developing questions, rather than stock transitions.
- Prefer concrete language. Remove empty declarations of importance, repeated summaries, and formulaic prose. Use lists for parallel material and tables for comparisons, not to replace connected reasoning with fragments.
- Preserve meaningful wording, attitudes, and uncertainty. Do not mechanically apply English word counts, grammar, or punctuation rules to Chinese prose.

## Lightweight review for every composed page

After editing, read the whole page, checking the new material's neighboring paragraphs, section openings and closings, and the ending:

1. Restate the article's question and the answer it reaches in one sentence. Check that they correspond.
2. Check whether premises appear when needed, references are clear, explanations are not repeated, and the article does not unexpectedly switch questions.
3. Compare the conclusion's scope with the material's support. Check for invented motives or positions.

Review against the intended reader's existing knowledge, not every possible background gap. Brief captures need not become articles to undergo review.

## Independent reader check

Use a fresh-context reader when the main line or section order changes, the audience includes unfamiliar readers, multiple dependent concepts are introduced, an experience is developed into a method or general judgment, or the editor cannot determine whether a major reasoning step is missing. Small additions of examples, corrections, and wording changes need only the lightweight review.

Give a fresh-context reader agent the current article, necessary background about the intended audience, and reading questions. Do not provide explanations from the author discussion or expected answers. Ask the reader to return:

- The question the article addresses and what it actually answers.
- Specific passages or section transitions that caused confusion, including the resulting misunderstanding.
- Missing premises and conclusions that exceed the article's supporting material.
- What is established, tentative, or unresolved in an exploratory article.

Reader testing checks understanding, not the truth of the author's beliefs. Evaluate and repair concrete gaps rather than relying on readability scores. After changing the main line, recheck the affected reading questions.
